Card-Not-Present (CNP) Fraud

Card-Not-Present (CNP) fraud occurs when a credit or debit card is used to make a purchase without the physical card being presented to the merchant. This type of fraud is common in online transactions, over-the-phone purchases, and mail orders.

Unlike traditional fraud where a thief physically steals a card, CNP fraud relies on obtaining card details through various means, such as data breaches, phishing scams, or malware.

How CNP Fraud Works

In e-commerce, CNP fraud typically involves these steps:

  • Obtaining Card Data: Fraudsters acquire cardholder information, which may include the card number, expiration date, CVV (Card Verification Value), and sometimes the cardholder's billing address.
  • Unauthorized Transaction: The fraudster uses this stolen information to make unauthorized purchases on online stores. Because the merchant cannot physically verify the cardholder, it's easier for fraudulent transactions to go undetected.
  • Delivery or Resale: The purchased goods may be shipped to an address controlled by the fraudster or resold.

Common Methods of Obtaining Card Data

  • Data Breaches: Large-scale data breaches at companies that store cardholder information can expose the details of thousands or even millions of customers to criminals.
  • Phishing: As discussed here, phishing scams can trick individuals into revealing their credit card information on fake websites.
  • Skimming: While primarily used in physical transactions, in some cases, malware on merchant websites can 'skim' card data entered by customers.
  • Social Engineering: Fraudsters may deceive individuals into providing their card details through social engineering tactics (e.g., posing as customer service representatives).
  • Malware: Malicious software installed on a user's computer or mobile device can capture card information entered during online transactions.

Examples of CNP Fraud in E-commerce

  • Unauthorized Purchases: The most common form, where a fraudster uses stolen card details to buy goods or services online.
  • Account Takeover: Fraudsters gain access to a customer's e-commerce account and make purchases using the stored payment information.
  • Triangulation Fraud (Related): While not strictly CNP, this fraud often involves CNP elements. A seller sets up a fake online store, collects orders and card details from customers, and then uses stolen card information to purchase the goods from a legitimate retailer, shipping them to the original customer. The fraudster disappears with the initial payments.

How to Prevent CNP Fraud (for Consumers)

  • Secure Your Devices: Keep your computer and mobile devices protected with up-to-date antivirus software.
  • Use Strong Passwords: Create strong, unique passwords for your online accounts.
  • Monitor Transactions: Regularly check your credit card statements and online account activity for any suspicious transactions.
  • Shop on Secure Sites: Only make purchases on websites that use HTTPS (look for the padlock icon in the address bar).
  • Be Wary of Phishing: Be cautious of unsolicited emails or messages asking for your credit card information.
  • Use Credit Cards Wisely: Credit cards generally offer better fraud protection than debit cards.
  • Consider Virtual Card Numbers: Some banks offer virtual card numbers for online shopping, which can limit the potential damage from fraud.
  • Enable Transaction Alerts: Set up alerts with your bank or credit card provider to notify you of any transactions.

How to Prevent CNP Fraud (for Businesses)

  • Address Verification System (AVS): Verify the cardholder's billing address.
  • Card Verification Value (CVV) Checks: Require customers to enter the CVV code.
  • Fraud Scoring Systems: Use systems that analyze transaction data to identify potentially fraudulent activity.
  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Implement MFA for customer accounts to add an extra layer of security.
  • 3D Secure: Use 3D Secure authentication (e.g., Verified by Visa, Mastercard SecureCode) to add an extra step of verification for online transactions.
  • Transaction Monitoring: Monitor transactions for suspicious patterns, such as unusually large orders or multiple orders from the same IP address.

By understanding the risks of Card-Not-Present fraud and taking appropriate precautions, both consumers and businesses can minimize their exposure to this type of online crime.
